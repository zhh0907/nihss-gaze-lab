Gaze Lab guide

Independent eye-position tracking with head-pose correction, video recording and a structured NIHSS item 2 review. Research prototype by Honghao Zhao.

Live camera

  1. Open over HTTPS, select Front or Rear camera, then Start camera. Allow camera access. Keep both eyes visible. Left/right refer to the patient.
  2. Turn up the sound and select Start gaze exam. The app announces an NIH Stroke Scale eye movement test, then asks the patient to look straight ahead, as far left as comfortable, and as far right as comfortable. Use suitable examiner targets; the phone edges do not define full gaze range.
  3. Recording stops automatically after speech plus movement and hold periods, usually about 30 seconds. A phase begins when its spoken cue starts; its hold begins after speech ends and movement time elapses. Browser events do not prove the sound was heard. Keep the page visible. A voice failure, early stop, hidden page or timing interruption preserves an incomplete clip and requires a new exam.
  4. After the local check, Eye motion shows A/B automatically. No manual eye or phase labels are needed for new guided clips. Download originals and data before closing or refreshing. Three clips are kept in memory; each is limited to five minutes or 256 MiB of chunks.

Preview orientation

The front-camera preview is flipped horizontally by default. In Tools → Setup → Recording & display settings, toggle Flip front-camera preview to change it while tracking. Eye markers follow the picture and L/R labels remain readable. Left/right measurements always refer to the subject. This display setting does not flip saved videos, imported videos or the rear camera. The app uses the active track’s reported facing mode when available and the selected mode otherwise. The input-unmirror option applies only to imported files.

Live tracking status

The preview shows eye markers while tracking. Routine counters and eye values are kept out of the live view; find measurements under Tools → Review → Tracking details. If updates pause, old eye positions are cleared and a delay message appears. A stalled browser frame callback switches to timer sampling automatically. If a capture error appears, stop and restart the camera. Runtime details are included in JSON exports for troubleshooting.

Video formats

In Setup, choose MP4 or WebM before recording. In Clips, Download format defaults to Original, preserving the recorded file. Select another supported format to convert locally, then tap Save when it is ready. Unsupported formats are disabled. Conversion takes roughly the clip duration; keep the tab visible. Cancel leaves the originals intact. Conversion re-encodes at up to 30 fps and can change quality and frame timing. Use original videos and JSON/CSV for timing analysis.

Import a phone video

Select a browser-supported MP4 or WebM of at most five minutes. If the original pixels are mirrored, enable the unmirror option. Preview the video and enter the actual exam intervals, one per line:

0 2 center
2 5 left
5 7 center
7 10 right

These are format examples, not an assumed protocol. Analyze video samples at 15 samples/sec by seeking. Change labels or parameters and analyze again. Missing intervals allow trajectories but not a complete exam summary.

Mobile clip controls and eye-only motion

Use Download and Delete at the top of each clip. Review, Eye motion and Video are separate views. Tap a track picture to select it. The selected picture is highlighted.

Eye motion shows fixed eye-corner lines and head-relative horizontal iris dots after your choices. It removes the head image and fixes line scale and orientation; the underlying pose correction remains approximate. Purple dots are accepted estimates. Missing measurements remain blank. The animation is not an angle measurement or a vertical-gaze test.

Automatic comparison shows A · Three positions and B · Full holds using original checked tracks with suspicious eye samples excluded. It does not use clinician findings or accepted replacements. Rest/Left/Right buttons only seek to recorded phases. No manual confirmation or method switching is required for new guided clips. Missing data cannot produce a normal result. These are experimental rules, not learned or validated NIHSS scores.

Optional segment review creates separate assisted evidence. Reviewed video export still requires segment decisions; original recordings remain downloadable. Independent clinician findings appear as an Examiner result. The animation leaves unreviewed suspicious eyes and unchecked samples blank. Older unlabeled imported videos retain manual controls because phase instructions cannot be reliably inferred from eye movement.

Method B: temporal assessment

B analyzes both eyes independently over the complete recorded holds, using elapsed time, robust endpoints, sustained movement and tracking coverage. It checks the end of each hold so missing extreme-position tracking cannot count as normal gaze or proven movement limitation. Blinks are excluded; prolonged or critical missing evidence causes abstention. A selects supported stable end-position windows. See method details. No clinical accuracy improvement is claimed.

Review suspicious segments

After recording, the clip is analysed locally as a sequence. Visible-iris ellipse evidence and image detector candidates are compared in head-relative coordinates with a bidirectional temporal fit. Only suspicious eye segments appear. Each segment shows the affected eye, with original and suggested tracks on the same archived camera frames. Use Play segment or the timeline to inspect the whole interval, then tap the Original or Suggested picture to choose. Eye closed marks this eye throughout the displayed segment as closed. Reviewed video and eye-only playback show Eye closed without a position dot; these samples are excluded from valid eye-position evidence. Original tracking and the other eye are unchanged. Unresolved keeps unsupported positions empty. Undo restores the unreviewed state.

Detected blinks are skipped when a high blink score coincides with lid closure and the eye reopens within 650 ms. Sustained closure and incomplete events at clip boundaries remain reviewable. Narrow lids alone are not treated as a blink because extreme gaze can also narrow the opening. Blink detection is imperfect. Blinks remain in the original video; they are not interpolated or presented as review tasks. The other eye is analysed independently.

Use suggested track applies available estimates only to suspicious samples in that eye segment. Any unresolved samples in the selected segment are displayed as unmeasured, never zero. Other samples and the other eye are retained. Estimates are purple. Nothing is accepted automatically, and original recordings and measurements are preserved.

Full video comparison switches between Original tracking and Reviewed tracking at the same playback time. The latter reflects your current choices. It redraws markers over the complete raw recording. Raw-video alignment is approximate; the segment preview instead uses the exact archived detector inputs. The Download button at the top of each clip offers available MP4/WebM encoders and review JSON/CSV. Complete segment choices before exporting reviewed results. Preparing a video snapshots your choices, takes roughly the clip duration, and needs the tab to remain visible. Tap Save after encoding. Original recording files and downloads remain available separately.

The method is experimental and has not been clinically validated. Visible-iris fitting can fail with eyelashes, reflections, light irises or weak image contrast. Temporal fits use image evidence and a bounded continuity cost, not an assumption that the eyes should move normally or symmetrically. Only short gaps bracketed by stable same-eye observations (at most 250 ms between anchors) can receive a temporal-only estimate; hidden movement is still possible. Longer invisible intervals, ambiguous fits, head loss, phase transitions and missing archive intervals do not receive such estimates. When a proposed centre is outside the visible lid opening, current iris evidence and same-eye observed anchors on both sides (at most 1.6 seconds apart) are required. The position must remain close to the observed range. These additional checks do not prove the hidden centre is correct. No three-dimensional eyeball calibration is claimed.

Clean-frame storage is limited to 48 MiB per clip and three pending encodes. Checked/total coverage is shown; unarchived samples remain untouched. Only captured analysis frames are checked, not every raw video frame. Older downloaded videos lack this archive; record a new clip for this workflow. Cancel and retry preserve originals. The clip suggestion is recomputed from review choices; the original session and independent examiner entries are preserved. No LLM or video upload is used. Download before refreshing or closing.

Unavailable eye positions

Each eye is displayed independently. A rejected eye shows — and a reason; its iris dot disappears and a dashed eye guide is labelled L ? or R ?. This means the position is unavailable, not centered. The other eye remains visible if valid. Traces have gaps for the affected eye, and filters restart on reacquisition. The recording uses the same rules. Exported rejected estimates are diagnostic data, not usable measurements. Both eyes still need adequate coverage for the examination summary. Extreme gaze and eyelid occlusion remain detector limitations; this display fix does not establish better detection accuracy.

Measurement and recording

MediaPipe face/iris landmarks are rectified onto an estimated head plane. Each eye is measured independently, normalized by half its eye-corner width. These values are not gaze angles. Raw 2D ratios remain available as a baseline. Resting deviation is retained; there is no initial-gaze zeroing or forced agreement between eyes.

Head pose, eye size, blinks, multiple faces and missing landmarks trigger quality checks. Defaults are research choices. Assumed camera intrinsics, lens distortion, occlusion and true 3D eye geometry still cause residual errors.

The annotated video uses the exact input frame associated with each result. Raw video preserves continuous motion; analysis frames are held between detections. JSON/CSV contain capture, analysis-display and estimated raw-video times on a shared browser clock, not hardware exposure timestamps. Both videos are silent. The raw clip preserves the original input orientation; analysis is unmirrored.

NIHSS review

The automatic layer extracts movement evidence. It does not assign a validated NIHSS score from eye positions. The review form maps examiner observations to item 2: normal horizontal gaze (0), partial gaze palsy and the applicable isolated peripheral nerve/deviation branches (1), or forced deviation/total paresis not overcome by the oculocephalic reflex (2). Missing or conflicting evidence returns Needs review. Reflex examination is for qualified clinicians; this app does not instruct neck maneuvers.

Enter an examiner ID and confirm an adequate exam. Video-supported review requires usable center/left/right evidence. Independent clinical observations are recorded as a separate evidence source. Tracking failure never implies a normal score. No patient validation or clinical accuracy guarantee is claimed.

Mobile use

The main screen keeps the camera and recording buttons visible. A short timer appears during recording; actionable errors or tracking interruptions appear only when needed. Secondary settings, traces, downloads and session exports are collapsed. Tools opens Setup, Clips, Review and Help. Close Tools to return to the live view. During a guided recording, Tools is disabled so the instructions remain visible. The preview fills the available space in portrait and landscape.

Use trusted HTTPS for the camera. Keep the app visible and the screen awake. Select the camera before starting. Recording is offered only when the browser supports both video recording and canvas capture. If unavailable, use a supported desktop browser or import an existing phone video. Device speed changes detection cadence; it does not change the quality thresholds.
