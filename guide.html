Gaze Lab guide

Independent eye-position tracking with head-pose correction, video recording and a structured NIHSS item 2 review. Research prototype by Honghao Zhao.

Live camera

  1. Open over HTTPS on a phone, select Front or Rear camera, then Start camera. Allow camera access. Keep one face in view with both eyes visible. Left and right refer to the subject.
  2. The examiner presents adequate center, left and right targets and selects each Live exam phase next to the preview. Phone screen edges do not define full gaze range.
  3. After detection starts, select Start recording. Stop recording finishes the clip while tracking continues. Stop camera also finishes recording.
  4. Under Recordings & replay, download the analysis video, raw video, eye JSON and eye CSV. Download before closing or refreshing. Up to three clips are kept in memory; each ends at five minutes or 256 MiB of encoded chunks.

Import a phone video

Select a browser-supported MP4 or WebM of at most five minutes. If the original pixels are mirrored, enable the unmirror option. Preview the video and enter the actual exam intervals, one per line:

0 2 center
2 5 left
5 7 center
7 10 right

These are format examples, not an assumed protocol. Analyze video samples at 15 samples/sec by seeking. Change labels or parameters and analyze again. Missing intervals allow trajectories but not a complete exam summary.

Measurement and recording

MediaPipe face/iris landmarks are rectified onto an estimated head plane. Each eye is measured independently, normalized by half its eye-corner width. These values are not gaze angles. Raw 2D ratios remain available as a baseline. Resting deviation is retained; there is no initial-gaze zeroing or forced agreement between eyes.

Head pose, eye size, blinks, multiple faces and missing landmarks trigger quality checks. Defaults are research choices. Assumed camera intrinsics, lens distortion, occlusion and true 3D eye geometry still cause residual errors.

The annotated video uses the exact input frame associated with each result. Raw video preserves continuous motion; analysis frames are held between detections. JSON/CSV contain capture, analysis-display and estimated raw-video times on a shared browser clock, not hardware exposure timestamps. Both videos are silent. The raw clip preserves the original input orientation; analysis is unmirrored.

NIHSS review

The automatic layer extracts movement evidence. It does not assign a validated NIHSS score from eye positions. The review form maps examiner observations to item 2: normal horizontal gaze (0), partial gaze palsy and the applicable isolated peripheral nerve/deviation branches (1), or forced deviation/total paresis not overcome by the oculocephalic reflex (2). Missing or conflicting evidence returns Needs review. Reflex examination is for qualified clinicians; this app does not instruct neck maneuvers.

Enter an examiner ID and confirm an adequate exam. Video-supported review requires usable center/left/right evidence. Independent clinical observations are recorded as a separate evidence source. Tracking failure never implies a normal score. No patient validation or clinical accuracy guarantee is claimed.

Mobile use

Use trusted HTTPS for the camera. Keep the app visible and the screen awake. Select the camera before starting. Recording is offered only when the browser supports both video recording and canvas capture. If unavailable, use a supported desktop browser or import an existing phone video. Device speed changes detection cadence; it does not change the quality thresholds.
