Gaze Lab guide

Independent eye tracking, recorded review and experimental NIHSS gaze evidence. Research prototype by Honghao Zhao; clinical assessment remains the examiner's responsibility.

Record an exam

  1. Open over HTTPS or localhost. Choose Front or Rear in Tools → Setup, then Start camera. Keep both eyes visible.
  2. Turn up the sound and select Start gaze exam. Follow the spoken straight-ahead, left and right instructions. Directions refer to the patient. The examiner should use suitable targets; phone edges do not define full gaze range.
  3. The center hold lasts 2.5 seconds; each side lasts 4 seconds. Total recording is usually 20–25 seconds including speech. Keep the page visible until “Test complete. You can relax.” finishes. Speech failure, early stopping or a hidden page preserves a partial recording.
  4. After the local check, open Clips → Eye motion. Rest/Left/Right plays each recorded phase. A complete score is not required for playback.

Browser speech events record when instructions played; they do not prove the patient heard or followed them. New guided clips need no manual phase labels. Original videos are silent.

Understand A and B

A · Three positions selects short, checked position windows representative of rest, left and right. B · Observation independently assesses sustained movement. A does not need B's longer window, and neither method requires a verified movement-onset time.

Bounded blinks and brief gaps need not invalidate otherwise supported measurements. Missing samples remain missing: they supply no invented positions or visible duration. The final position and later changes still matter; an earlier clean interval cannot hide an unresolved endpoint.

Partial · N/3 phases means some evidence is available but no complete score is supported. Details keeps the usable individual-eye values and each method's reasons. A missing eye is not treated as centered. “No usable evidence” is not a normal score or proof of palsy.

Automatic results use original checked tracks with flagged measurements excluded. Doctor-selected corrections produce a separate reviewed result. Independent clinician findings appear as an Examiner result. The original baseline never learns from a selection.

Original-track timing is a separate diagnostic. An observed stable time can remain visible when onset timing is uncertain. These timings do not control A/B eligibility. Complete evidence may yield Possible 0, Possible 1 or Review 1–2; these are unvalidated research suggestions.

Review an eye segment

Only suspicious eyes appear in Review. Play the segment, then tap the preferred picture: Keep original or Use suggested. Both need correction saves a later-annotation task without accepting either candidate. Eye closed removes the position dot for that eye throughout the segment. Undo reverses the choice. The other eye and original recording are preserved.

Confirmed blinks are skipped as review tasks; sustained closure remains reviewable. Suggested positions require image or bounded same-eye temporal support. Unsupported positions remain blank. Nothing is accepted automatically, and changing a track does not retrain a model.

Eye motion merges retained originals and accepted replacements into one track with uniform purple dots. The diagram fixes eye-corner line width and removes the head image. Values are normalized horizontal positions, not calibrated eye angles. Pose correction remains approximate. Video provides separate Original tracking and Reviewed tracking playback at the same time.

Save recordings and choices

Use the clip's Download action for original video, reviewed video, eye-only video and JSON/CSV. Complete segment choices before exporting a reviewed video. Choose an available MP4/WebM format, prepare the video, then tap Save. Conversion takes roughly the clip duration and can change quality or timing; use originals and timestamps for precise analysis.

For deferred work, choose Download → Save annotation package → Save ZIP. It contains the original video, archived images, candidate tracks and choice history, without NIHSS scores. It is a handoff for later annotation, not an in-app point editor.

Download before refreshing or closing. This public edition keeps clips in browser memory. It has no automatic cloud upload, persistent queue, LLM or online training. Limited image storage can leave unchecked samples; missing archive images are not approved evidence. JSON retains quality checks, independent A/B selections and partial results separately from review and clinician findings.

Camera and phone use

The front-camera preview is mirrored by default; the rear preview is natural. Setup → Recording & display settings can change the front preview. This display setting does not flip saved videos or swap the anatomical eyes. Imported mirrored videos have a separate unmirror setting.

The main phone screen keeps the camera and recording actions visible. Tools contains Setup, Clips, Review and Help. Keep the app visible and screen awake. If tracking stalls, old positions disappear and a delay message appears; stop and restart if capture fails. Device speed affects detection cadence.

Import a video

Select a supported MP4/WebM of at most five minutes. For an unlabelled video, enter the actual instructed intervals, for example:

0 2 center
2 5 left
5 8 right

These are syntax examples, not assumed timing. Do not label direction from eye motion alone. Older unlabelled clips retain manual position/interval controls; historical guided clips retain their recorded analysis strategy.

Limits and references

Extreme gaze, eyelid occlusion, reflections, head motion and camera geometry can still cause errors. Resting deviation is retained without initial-gaze zeroing. Tracking failure cannot establish normal gaze, palsy or a reflex result. Applicable clinical examination findings must come from the examiner; this app does not instruct reflex maneuvers.

Synthetic and browser tests verify software behavior, not clinical accuracy or physical iPhone/Safari performance.
